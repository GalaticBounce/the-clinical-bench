# The introducer model: exposures, fee options and what changes

Written 11 September 2026 after Jamie's direction that Clinical Bench may operate purely
as an introducer: it finds and connects, charges for the search and the connection, and
the clinician contracts directly with the company. Clinicians do not work for Clinical
Bench. This note re-runs `LIABILITY-REVIEW.md` under that model and sets out the fee
structures worth exploring. Desk research by a non-lawyer, sources linked. Not legal
advice. Not served publicly.

## 1. What the model does to the six exposures

| Exposure | Under the contracting model (current site copy) | Under the introducer model |
|---|---|---|
| Labour hire licensing | The largest exposure: Clinical Bench contracts with and pays the clinician and supplies them to a client | **Largely removed.** Queensland excludes recruitment and placement services from labour hire because the placement business does not pay the worker ([Labour Hire Licensing Queensland](https://www.labourhire.qld.gov.au/licensing), [HIA summary](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/managing-your-business/managing-compliance/articles/labour-hire-licensing)). Victoria treats recruitment as labour hire only where the provider also procures accommodation, or recruits contractors and then manages the contract ([Labour Hire Authority](https://www.labourhireauthority.vic.gov.au/provider/key-industries/recruitment-and-placement-services/)). The condition is that Clinical Bench never pays the clinician and never manages the engagement |
| Superannuation guarantee | Real and retrospective | **Gone for Clinical Bench.** The company pays the clinician; any SG question is the company's. Worth a line in the company terms so nobody later argues Clinical Bench should have warned them |
| Uninsured advice | Clinical Bench is the contracting party and carries the report | **Reduced to the introduction itself.** Clinical Bench warrants nothing about the work, only that it checked the register on a given date. It still needs professional indemnity for what it does say (registration checked, discipline matched) and public liability |
| Endorsement law | Contractual through Clinical Bench's client terms | **Same law, weaker lever.** The company and clinician contract directly, so the no-endorsement rule has to live in the platform terms both accept and in the engagement template Clinical Bench provides |
| No contracts, no terms, no entity | Certain | **Still certain, different documents.** Platform terms for companies, platform terms for clinicians, and a template engagement agreement between them |
| Privacy | Patient data inside AI evaluation work | **Moved to the company and clinician.** Clinical Bench holds application and enquiry data only. It should never hold project data. Keep it that way by design |

Two exposures are **new** under the introducer model, and both come from charging for
introductions:

### 1a. Employment agent rules on fees charged to the person seeking work

Introducing people to work for a fee is regulated as private employment agency in
several states, and the recurring rule is that the person seeking work must not be
charged.

- **Queensland.** Anyone who, for gain, offers to find casual, temporary, permanent or
  **contract work** for a person, or a contract worker for a person, is a private
  employment agent. No licence, but the Code of Conduct applies, including the fee
  restriction and a mandatory information statement to every work seeker
  ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/service-industries-professionals/service-industries/private-employment-agents)).
  The Code provides that an agent must not seek or accept a fee, deposit or bond from a
  work seeker as a condition of finding or attempting to find work, with narrow
  exceptions for models and performers
  ([Code of Conduct Regulation 2015](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0093)).
  The mandatory information statement agents must give work seekers puts it plainly: "We
  will not and must not charge you any fee for finding, or attempting to find, work for
  you" ([example statement as published by a Queensland agency](https://www.davidsonwp.com/information-statement-for-queensland-work-seekers)).
  Whether a registered clinician taking a commercial engagement is a "work seeker" is the
  question for the lawyer; the Act's definition reaches contract work.
- **New South Wales.** No licence, but an employment placement service must not
  "charge a job seeker a fee for the purpose of finding the job seeker employment"
  ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/industry-specific-business-requirements/running-an-employment-placement-service)).
  Whether a clinician taking contract work is a "job seeker" is not settled on that page.
- **Western Australia.** A licence is required to run an agency that charges a fee to
  help people find work or to help businesses find workers, "even if the worker does not
  pay you directly" ([WA Government](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/employment-agents-licence)).
  Whether an online business outside WA introducing a clinician to a WA company needs
  one is not answered on that page. Ask before taking a WA engagement.
- **Victoria, SA, ACT, Tasmania, NT.** No general employment agent licence found. Consumer
  law applies.

Market practice cuts both ways. Airtasker charges the tasker a service fee of 12.5 to
20 per cent ([Airtasker](https://support.airtasker.com/hc/en-au/articles/900003895046-What-are-Tasker-tiers)),
so contractor-side fees clearly exist in Australia. Expert360 charges the client a
platform fee of roughly 15 to 20 per cent on top of the consultant's rate
([Expert360 comparison](https://expertstackhub.ai/insights/glg-vs-expert360)), and
MDisrupt charges the company a platform access fee. The professional-services platforms
charge the buyer. That is the safer pattern here, and it is also the better commercial one.

### 1b. Misleading conduct about the introduction

Under the introducer model the only thing Clinical Bench sells is the quality of the
match and the truth of what it says about the clinician. "Registration checked",
"screened for commercial experience", "matched in days" are each a representation under
section 18 of the Australian Consumer Law. The protection is a written process behind
each claim and a dated record of every check.

## 2. Fee structures worth exploring

| Model | How it works | Who pays | Legal note | Commercial note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Search fee** | Flat fee per brief to run the search and present shortlisted, register-checked clinicians. Payable whether or not the company proceeds | Company | Clean everywhere. A service fee for a service | Low friction to quote; caps upside; companies may balk before they trust the bench |
| **B. Connection or success fee** | Fee when an introduction becomes an engagement. Flat, or a percentage of the agreed engagement value | Company | Clean, provided Clinical Bench does not handle the clinician's money. Needs non-circumvention terms and a clear trigger | Aligns with value; the standard expert-network model; percentage needs the engagement value disclosed by the company |
| **C. Search fee credited against success fee** | A modest search fee, credited in full against the success fee if an engagement follows | Company | As A and B | Filters tyre-kickers without punishing serious buyers. **Recommended starting point** |
| **D. Percentage from the clinician** | Clinician pays a share of what they earn from the introduction | Clinician | **Risky in Queensland and NSW** under the work-seeker fee rules, and needs a licence in WA. Even if contract work falls outside those rules, the first complaint to a regulator would be expensive to answer | Depresses supply, which is the scarce side. Not recommended |
| **E. Clinician subscription or listing fee** | Clinicians pay to be on the bench | Clinician | Same problem as D in a different shape | Kills supply. Not recommended |
| **F. Company subscription** | Annual access to the bench plus a reduced connection fee | Company | Clean | Later, once there is a bench worth subscribing to |

**Recommendation to explore first: model C.** A search fee of a few hundred dollars per
brief, credited against a success fee of 15 to 20 per cent of the agreed engagement
value, payable by the company on signing the engagement. Clinician side free. This keeps
Clinical Bench out of the payment flow, out of the labour hire definitions, out of the
work-seeker fee rules, and pays it for the two things it actually does: the search and the
match.

## 3. Rules that keep the model clean

1. **Never touch the clinician's money.** The company pays the clinician directly.
   Clinical Bench invoices its own fee separately. No escrow, no pass-through, no
   collecting on the clinician's behalf. Paying the worker is the trigger in every labour
   hire definition.
2. **Never manage the engagement.** Clinical Bench introduces, provides a template
   engagement agreement, and steps back. It does not set hours, direct the work, review
   the deliverable or hold project data. Managing contract performance is Victoria's
   trigger for contractor arrangements.
3. **Never charge the clinician.** For the fee rules above and for supply.
4. **Say what was checked, and when.** The register check is dated and recorded. The
   platform terms say it is a point-in-time check of a public register, not a warranty.
5. **Keep the no-endorsement rule in three places:** company terms, clinician terms, and
   the engagement template.
6. **Scope and fee are agreed between company and clinician in writing before work
   starts.** Clinical Bench can supply the template and can help write the brief, and
   that help is part of the search service, not management of the work.

## 5. What changes in the contracts

`CONTRACT-TERM-SHEETS.md` was written for the contracting model. Under the introducer
model the three documents become:

1. **Platform terms for companies.** The service (search, shortlist, introduction,
   template); the fees (model C); when the success fee is payable and how engagement
   value is declared; non-circumvention for 12 months on any introduced clinician; no
   endorsement; Clinical Bench is not a party to the engagement and warrants only the
   dated register check; the company is responsible for its own contract, payment,
   superannuation position, data handling and insurance; liability capped at fees paid
   with an s64A limitation for the consumer guarantees.
2. **Platform terms for clinicians.** Free to join and free to use; registration and
   insurance warranties with a duty to notify changes; conduct rules (no endorsement, no
   patient data outside the client's systems, conflicts declared); the Queensland
   information statement for work seekers supplied at onboarding; non-circumvention
   mirrored; removal from the bench on breach.
3. **Engagement template** between company and clinician, supplied as a courtesy with a
   line saying it is a template and each party should take its own advice: scope,
   deliverable, fee, timing, confidentiality, no endorsement, reliance statement, data,
   IP, termination.

## 6. Questions for the lawyer, in order

1. Under model C, are we outside the labour hire definitions in Queensland and Victoria,
   and does supplying an engagement template or helping write a brief count as managing
   the engagement?
2. Is a registered clinician taking a fixed-scope commercial engagement a "work seeker"
   under the Queensland Code and a "job seeker" under the NSW provision? If yes, model D
   is closed. If no, it is still not worth it.
3. Do we need a WA employment agent licence to introduce a clinician to a WA company from
   Queensland, and if so is it worth holding one?
4. Is the s64A limitation in the company terms drafted correctly?
5. Does the Queensland Code's information statement obligation apply to us, and if so
   what must the onboarding flow show?
